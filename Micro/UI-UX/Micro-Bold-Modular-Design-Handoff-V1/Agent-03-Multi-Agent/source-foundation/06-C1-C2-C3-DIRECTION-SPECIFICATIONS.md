# C1, C2, and C3 Direction Specifications

## Shared contract

All three concepts are variants of **Bold Modular Micro**. They share:

- The same product architecture.
- The same Arabic copy and figures.
- The same primary viewport.
- The same required states.
- The same accessibility bar.
- The same core interaction model.

They differ in visual temperament, color balance, modular expression, and perceived energy.

Do not combine them until each has been presented and evaluated independently.

## C1 — Warm Bold

### Intent

The closest direction to home-based and craft businesses without becoming rustic, handmade, or nostalgic.

### Visual emphasis

- A vivid terracotta/coral family leads.
- Warm neutrals remain but no full-screen beige wash.
- Trust blue is secondary and controlled.
- Rounded modular fields feel human but not soft or childish.
- Primary actions feel energetic and obvious.

### Borrowed strength

Direction A contributes warmth, approachability, and plain-language comfort.

### Required proof

- Demonstrate that warmth does not reduce financial authority.
- Show a negative-result state that remains serious.
- Show readable long-session finance surfaces.

### Primary risk

Becoming a craft-market or food-delivery aesthetic instead of a financial operating system.

## C2 — Confident Bold

### Intent

A modern financial-control direction with visible energy and strong trust, avoiding generic blue fintech.

### Visual emphasis

- Deep blue or blue-teal carries trust and active context.
- Warm brand color carries create/start energy.
- Cleaner neutral canvas and stronger white/tonal surfaces.
- Numeric hierarchy is highly disciplined.
- Color blocks are architectural rather than decorative.

### Borrowed strength

Direction B contributes traceability, restraint in finance, and professional confidence.

### Required proof

- Demonstrate that the warm identity remains recognizable.
- Avoid looking like a bank or enterprise SaaS.
- Show how home is energetic while records remain calm.

### Primary risk

Becoming a polished but generic global finance interface.

## C3 — Dynamic Modular

### Intent

The highest acceptable expression level for Micro: strong modular differentiation, bold status, and visible interactivity.

### Visual emphasis

- Strong color zones identify task and state.
- Work, finance, tools, and future market may have controlled contextual accents.
- Status/progress language is prominent.
- Motion concepts are more visible but remain functional.
- The interface feels active before any user action.

### Required proof

- Demonstrate one coherent product identity despite module differentiation.
- Show finance without color overload.
- Show that a low-experience user can still identify the first action.
- Provide color-blind and grayscale checks for critical status.

### Primary risk

Fragmentation, rainbow navigation, cognitive overload, or a “vibe-coded” appearance.

## Comparison requirements

For each direction, include:

1. One-sentence strategic thesis.
2. Five brand adjectives.
3. Mood references by principle, not screenshots to copy.
4. Light and dark palette roles.
5. Contrast table.
6. Typography scale.
7. Surface and elevation logic.
8. Icon treatment.
9. Business Status Hero.
10. Micro Signal.
11. Quick Action Bar.
12. Success Impact storyboard.
13. Required screens.
14. Three strengths.
15. Three risks.
16. Elements requiring user testing.

## Fair-comparison rules

- Use identical Arabic text and amounts.
- Use identical screen widths and crop boundaries.
- Use the same number of screens and states.
- Do not add charts, illustrations, or features to only one direction.
- Do not polish one direction beyond the others.
- Keep direction names hidden in quick user preference tests when possible.
