01

The mark and what it means

Four soft keystone forms — one shape rotated four times — around a square centre that stays open. Flat colour, generous radii, no gradient, no outline, no effect.

Short rationale, safe to publish: four kinds of everyday business events, drawn as one careful form and held apart by an even space, around a centre that stays open — because the owner completes the picture, not the software.

Longer version, for the product team: Micro's job is to keep separate things separate — events, consequences, what is known, what is missing — while showing how they relate. The mark is built that way: one construction repeated, so the parts are visibly one family; an even channel, so nothing merges; and an open centre that is a construction element, not a hole. The tone comes from the material rather than from decoration, so the mark survives a 60px icon, a thermal receipt and an embroidered apron equally.

No origin story is claimed. The four forms carry no fixed meaning. They are not four modules, not four accounts, not Manage / Services / Market / Delivery, and none of them is ever extracted as a sub-brand or module icon.

Before · original reference
Four separately drawn forms. Radii, tapers and areas disagree; the channel is uneven; the neutral is too pale for small sizes; raster only.
After · developed
One unit on a 192-unit grid, rotated 0/90/180/270°. Even channel, open square centre, optical weight correction per colour, clean vector paths, plus a compact variant.
One unit, four rotations
Every form is the same six-point keystone with the same radius profile, so the variety reads as rhythm rather than inconsistency.
Blunt inner ends
Tips are cut flat with a small radius instead of coming to a point. This is what stops the mark reading as a spinner or a flower.
Optical weight correction
Dark form ×1.025, light form ×0.975. Mathematically unequal so they look equal — the correction lives in the file, not in the eye.
Contrast-safe neutral
Warm taupe #B79C86 at 2.4:1 on ivory holds down to 40px. A paler neutral disappears at small sizes.

Construction — every number

Grid. A 192 × 192 unit square on 24-unit modules. The drawn ink occupies the inner 168 units (87.5%) — 12 units of built-in margin on every side. This matters in practice: dropping the file into a 36 × 36px frame renders the mark at about 31.5px, below the 32px minimum. Size the frame from the ink, not the artboard, or use the compact variant.

The unit. One six-point keystone, built by rounding a polygon. The polygon corners — the construction input, not points on the finished curve — are, clockwise from top-left: 50,12 · 142,12 · 133,60 · 108,74 · 84,74 · 59,60. Corner radii, same order: 30 · 30 · 15 · 9 · 9 · 15. Rounding pulls each corner inward, so the curve's own anchors land elsewhere — the top edge, for instance, runs from about 54.7 to 137.3. To reproduce the shape exactly, use the path below rather than re-deriving it from these numbers. Rotated 90°, 180° and 270° about 96,96.

Open centre. A 44 × 44 unit square, about 23% of the mark's width. It is a construction element: never fill it, never place a glyph in it, never let a pattern read through it as texture.

Clear space. X = 16 units, one sixth of a form's width. Inner dashed box is X (absolute minimum), outer is 2X (preferred). Measured from the 192-unit artboard edge, not from the visible ink — since the ink is already inset 12 units, the true clearance around the forms is X + 12.

Optical correction. Light backgrounds: ink ×1.025, taupe ×0.975, terracotta and turquoise ×1.0. Dark inverts: ivory ×0.975, warm brown ×1.02. Do not normalise these — they are deliberate.

Minimum sizes. Primary mark 32px · compact variant 16–32px · app icon 40px rendered · below 16px a single-colour terracotta silhouette.

05

App icon and size behaviour

The icon is the mark, not a redrawing of it. The only differences are a slightly widened channel and slightly smaller forms, so the shape survives the sizes a phone actually renders.

Inside the product · transparent
In the app interface — header, empty states, in-page marks — the mark sits on a transparent background and inherits the surface beneath it, exactly as the repository specifies. Use micro-quad.svg on light surfaces and micro-quad-dark.svg on dark.
As an app icon · opaque, always
An app icon must have a filled field: iOS rejects transparency outright, and Android composites the transparent area onto an unpredictable colour. The three icon files below are therefore opaque by design. This is not a contradiction of the transparent rule — it is a different surface with a different requirement.

Honest reading: excellent down to 60px, good at 32px, and at 16px the mark becomes a warm coloured cluster rather than four readable forms. Accept that — a favicon is recognised by colour and silhouette, not detail — and use the compact variant everywhere below 32px.
Home-screen behaviour
The grey-shape tiles stand in for the user's other apps. Against neighbouring icons the ivory field is the quietest and the terracotta field the most findable. Ship ivory as the default, keep terracotta as the alternate for launch campaigns, and never ship both to the same user.
Platform geometry — already applied in the delivered files
iOS. Mark at 62% of the icon square on a filled field, corner radius left to the system. Sizes 1024 · 180 · 152 · 120 · 76 · 60 · 40.
Android adaptive. 432 × 432 foreground with the mark at 60% so it survives circular, squircle and rounded masks, plus a flat background layer.
Web. Maskable 512 at 58% for PWA safe-zone cropping; favicon 48 · 32 · 16 on an ivory field, plus a multi-size favicon.ico.
06

Motion — the mark

Allowed — motion that resolves
Forms may fly in, rotate into place or settle inward, as long as the animation ends, decelerates, and stops on the locked mark. 600–900ms total, 60–80ms stagger, ease-out, no bounce, no overshoot beyond 2%.
Forbidden — motion that never ends
Never a continuous rotation, never a loading spinner, never a looping pulse of the whole mark. A looping mark turns the identity into a status indicator.
A · Spin that lands
900ms
The whole mark turns in from −300° and settles with a 6° overshoot. The most confident option, and my recommendation for the splash screen.
B · Gather from outside
760ms
Each form slides in from its own side, 60ms apart. The clearest expression of separate things arriving into one picture.
C · Sequence
880ms
Each form turns 90° into its own place, one after another. Deliberate and slow — good for a first-launch moment, too slow for everyday use.
D · Settle inward
640ms
The forms contract inward from beyond the frame. The quietest of the four — right inside the app, where the mark appears often.
The loader — the one legitimate loop

It works precisely because nothing rotates. Emphasis travels around the four forms while every form stays exactly where the locked mark puts it. Only opacity and a 6% scale move; the geometry never does. 1600ms cycle, 200ms stagger, resting at 18% opacity.

For a smaller loader, use a single form pulsing in place — never the four-form mark spinning.

Motion — the lockup with the name

The rule is always the same: the mark resolves first, then the word arrives. The word never animates before the mark, never moves after the mark has settled, and never animates letter by letter.

#D59172 · #5EC0C1 · #8C7A66 · #FFF7ED on #1C1917. Measured contrast 6.8:1, 8.2:1, 4.3:1 and 16.6:1. The ink form becomes warm white and the taupe form becomes warm brown, so light and dark keep the same weight distribution. Note that #8C7A66 is a second logo-only neutral and must be registered alongside #B79C86 — it is the dark-mode counterpart, not an interface colour.
Alignment with the Micro repository
Three of the four logo colours are the product's own tokens, unchanged: brand-primary #CC785C, accent-primary #079FA0, text-primary #1F1E1D, on canvas #FAF9F5. Never substitute a different hex for these — read them from the tokens.
The fourth is new and deliberately so. Register two logo-only values: brand-neutral #B79C86 for light backgrounds and brand-neutral-dark #8C7A66 for dark. Neither may be used for interface surfaces, borders or text. The existing interface neutrals were tested and rejected for the mark: divider #DAD5C8 measures about 1.2:1 on canvas and border #EAE6DC even less, so the fourth form would vanish at icon sizes. #B79C86 is the nearest value in the same warm family that survives 40px.
The repository's own rule — brand colour is not status colour — holds here without exception. Income, expense, withdrawal and return colours are never logo colours, and no logo colour ever carries a financial meaning. One further boundary: Instrument Sans belongs to the logo lockup only and must never enter the product interface, which is typeset in IBM Plex Sans Arabic and IBM Plex Mono. Keeping it out of the UI prevents a third typeface drifting into the codebase.

Correct and incorrect use

Correct
Full colour on ivory, clear space respected.
Correct
Dark set on the dark field, never the light set.
Correct
One colour for stamps, engraving, low-grade print.
Correct
Compact variant below 32px, never the primary.
Never
Rotate or tilt the static mark, or loop it as a spinner.
Never
Stretch, squash, or rebuild the proportions.
Never
Fill the open centre or place a glyph in it.
Never
Gradients, shadows, glows, or a busy photo behind it.
Product-specific prohibitions. The mark never labels a product area — not Manage, not Services, not Market, not Delivery — and none of its four forms is ever extracted as a sub-brand or module icon. It is never a status indicator: no green version, no red version, no version meaning "complete".
Colour discipline
  • Brand colour never encodes financial meaning. Success, warning, error, review and incomplete keep their own semantic set, visibly different from these values.
  • No colour in the mark represents a product area, an account or a number.
  • The four colours are never recoloured for a campaign.
  • Terracotta and turquoise are never a good/bad pair.

Files — what to use when

SVG · the master source
Always start here for anything new. Plain vector paths, no filters, no raster, no embedded fonts, no external references. Opens and edits cleanly in Figma, Illustrator, Inkscape, Sketch, and any browser. Infinitely scalable — favicon to shop sign.
PDF · for printers
True vector PDF, curves only, no text objects and no fonts to go missing. Send this to any print shop, signmaker or embroiderer. Full colour and one-colour ink. Ask for a proof before a long run.
PNG · ready to use
For places that will not take a vector: app stores, social profiles, slide decks, documents, email signatures. Transparent for the mark, filled field for the icons. Never scale a PNG up — go back to the SVG.

Still your decision
Three items only, and none of them can be settled from inside this document. Pantone equivalents — needed only if you print with spot colours, and they must be chosen against a physical swatch book at your printer, never from a screen. A trademark similarity search — four-part radial marks are common enough that this should be done by a trademark attorney before any registration. Whether print, packaging or signage is in scope this year — your commercial decision; the vector and PDF masters are ready either way.
